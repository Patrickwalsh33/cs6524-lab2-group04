\section{Inter-frame Pose and Depth Estimation}
\label{sec:depth}

We pair each frame with the frame 10 steps later and match SIFT points
between them. From these matches we get the essential matrix $E$, and from that, we get the camera's rotation
$R$ and direction of movement $t$. We tried computing $E$ from $F$ as in the brief, but it gave more
bad results, so we compute $E$ directly. We skip pairs where the camera turned more than 5$^\circ$ or
did not move mostly sideways.

Each pair is rectified so that matching points lie on the same row in both images.
StereoSGBM then finds how far each pixel shifted between the two images (the disparity $d$), and
depth is $Z = fB/d$. The camera motion only gives a direction, not a distance, so we assume the
camera moved at a speed of 15\,cm/s, giving $B = 5$\,cm. Fig.~\ref{fig:depth} shows the result.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig_depth.png}
\caption{Frame and depth map for 01-02 (left) and 02-02 (right). Black means no depth was found.}
\label{fig:depth}
\end{figure*}

For the point cloud, we take one frame from the middle of each video and turn every pixel with a
depth into a coloured 3D point. We drop points closer than 20\,cm or further than 5\,m, and points
whose depth is very different from their neighbours, since those are probably errors at object edges
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cloud}).

\subsection{Discussion and Failure Analysis}

The biggest problem is that the cm scale is a guess. One camera cannot tell how far it moved, so every depth depends on
our assumed 15\,cm/s. Hand speed also varies, so depths jump between frames: the bench in 01-01 reads
about 100\,cm in one frame and 200\,cm in another.

Neighbouring frames were also too close together. The camera moves only about 0.5\,cm between frames, so
pixels barely shift and the results were unreliable. We mostly fixed this by using frames that were 10 frames apart.

Correcting lens distortion made things worse. Our distortion values are unrealistically large
(Table~\ref{tab:calib}), and correcting with them gave fewer usable pairs, so we skipped that.

Finally, the left edge of each depth map is always empty, because those pixels have no
match in the other image; this is why the shoe in 02-01 has no depth. Glass and plain walls in 01-01
have nothing to match, people walk through 01-02, and our 02 scenes point at the floor.
