\section{Camera Calibration}

Each calibration video has around 200 frames, many of them blurry or nearly identical, so we pick
20. Every frame is first checked with a quick \texttt{findChessboardCorners} pass to make sure the
whole board is visible, and given a sharpness score, the variance of its Laplacian, since motion
blur weakens edges. The video is then split into 20 equal chunks and the sharpest frame in each is
kept. Since the board was moving the whole time, frames from different chunks show it from different
angles. Our first version threw away the blurrier half of the frames before choosing, which left
some chunks empty. The gaps were filled with five back-to-back frames from the start of the video,
which add almost nothing.

On the chosen frames we run the full corner detection and refine each corner with
\texttt{cornerSubPix} in an $11\times11$ window. The squares are only about 20\,px wide at our
resolution, so a bigger window could reach into the next square. The matching 3D points are the
$8\times5$ grid $(2.5i, 2.5j, 0)$\,cm on the board plane. Using cm here means that once we have a
baseline in cm, depth also comes out in cm. \texttt{calibrateCamera} then fits $K$ and the
distortion coefficients using the standard pinhole model with radial and tangential
distortion~\cite{foundationsCVbook}. We report the mean distance between the detected corners and
the reprojected ones rather than the RMS value that \texttt{calibrateCamera} returns. If the mean
were above 0.5\,px, the code would drop the worst frame and fit again, but this never happened.

Table~\ref{tab:calib} shows the results. $K$ looks sensible for all three phones: $f_x \approx f_y$,
the principal point is within about 10\,px of the image centre (180, 320), and skew is 0 because
OpenCV assumes it by default. Cam~03 has a longer focal length, giving about a 37$^\circ$ horizontal
field of view against 43$^\circ$ for the other two, so its main camera is simply a bit narrower.

The distortion coefficients are a different story. Every error is well below the 0.5\,px target,
but $k_2$ and $k_3$ are huge ($k_3$ reaches $-115$ for Cam~01) and change sign from one phone to
the next, which real phone lenses would not do. When we checked where the detected corners fell, they only covered the middle of the frame
(roughly $x$ 105--280 and $y$ 250--383 out of $360\times640$ for Cam~01), because we held the board
at about the same distance throughout and it never got near the edges. Distortion matters most at the
edges, so the fit is free to pick any high-order terms that happen to match the centre. Fixing
$k_3 = 0$ shows this: the error stayed exactly the same while $k_2$ jumped to compensate. A low
reprojection error therefore only tells us the model fits the corners we gave it, not that it is
right across the whole image. For this reason the depth stage uses $K$ alone and does not undistort
the frames (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth}).
