\section{Introduction}
The aim of this lab was to measure how far away things are using just one phone camera. We first
calibrated each camera to find its intrinsic matrix $K$. Then we filmed a scene while sliding the
camera sideways, so that two frames taken a moment apart work like a pair of stereo cameras. Each
of us used our own phone, giving three cameras and six scenes: camera 01 is an iPhone, and cameras
02 and 03 are Android phones.

\section{Data Acquisition}
All videos were filmed in portrait with focus and exposure locked, and each phone's calibration
and scene videos were filmed with the same lens. The checkerboard has $9\times6$ squares of
2.5\,cm. OpenCV looks for the inner corners, so the pattern size is $8\times5$; using $9\times6$
from the brief found nothing.

To fit the 5\,MB limit, every video was scaled down to $360\times640$. Since $K$ is in pixels, each
camera's scene videos had to be scaled exactly the same way as its calibration video. Camera 03
recorded at $1080\times1920$ and was scaled to one third; cameras 01 and 02 were
halved from $720\times1280$.
